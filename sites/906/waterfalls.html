Frequently Asked

UP Waterfalls FAQ

When is the best time of year to visit UP waterfalls?
Late April through early June is peak flow from snowmelt — the falls are at their most thunderous. Mid-September through mid-October pairs solid water with fall color. Summer is the easiest for hiking and swimming below smaller falls. Winter offers spectacular ice formations at Tahquamenon, Munising, and Bond — but most access roads aren't plowed past the parking lots.
Do I need a Michigan Recreation Passport for UP waterfalls?
Yes for state park falls (Tahquamenon, Porcupine Mountains/Manabezho) — $14/year for Michigan residents, $40 for non-residents. National Park Service falls in Pictured Rocks (Munising, Miners, Sable) require the standard NPS entrance fee or an America the Beautiful pass. The Black River Falls and most others are free.
Which UP waterfalls are wheelchair / stroller accessible?
Upper Tahquamenon Falls has a paved viewing area accessible by paved path from the parking lot. Munising Falls has a paved path to the lower viewing platform. Bond Falls has a wooden boardwalk loop with multiple viewing platforms. Most other falls require some unpaved trail walking or stairs.
Can I swim at UP waterfalls?
A few yes, most no. Lower Tahquamenon has popular swimming holes below the cascades. Many of the smaller Pictured Rocks creeks have wadeable pools. Tahquamenon Upper Falls, Bond, and the Black River falls are off-limits to swimming due to currents and drop-offs. Always check posted signs.
What's the best base town for a UP waterfall trip?
For Eastern UP (Tahquamenon): Paradise or Newberry. For Central UP (Pictured Rocks waterfalls): Munising. For Western UP (Bond, Black River, Porcupines): Bergland, Bessemer, or Ontonagon. Marquette works as a base for everything except the deepest western falls.
