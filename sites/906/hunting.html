Season So Far

Harvest and wildlife numbers

Michigan's mandatory harvest reporting gives a near-real-time picture of the deer season, and the DNR's grouse and woodcock cooperator survey gives something even better for small game: county-level flushes per hour, reported by hunters, updated as the season runs.

We link to the DNR's own numbers rather than republishing them. Reported harvest totals are preliminary — they are what hunters have entered so far, not the DNR's final harvest estimate, and they lag the actual kill by up to 72 hours because that's the reporting window. We are not scraping eLicense pages to build our own tallies. If the DNR makes a supported feed available, we'll ask first and wire it in properly.

Frequently Asked

UP Hunting FAQ

What are legal hunting hours in the Upper Peninsula?
For deer, bear, fall turkey, furbearers and most small game: one-half hour before sunrise to one-half hour after sunset. Woodcock is different — sunrise to sunset. Regular waterfowl is different again — one-half hour before sunrise to sunset, with no evening half hour. Early teal is sunrise to sunset. And if you're hunting Gogebic, Iron, Dickinson or Menominee counties you're on Central Time, which is the single most common way an out-of-town hunter ends up shooting outside legal light. The card at the top of this page computes all of it for whichever county you pick.
Can I still bait deer in the UP?
Yes — the Upper Peninsula is the only part of Michigan where you can. Baiting runs Sept. 15 through Jan. 1, capped at 2 gallons per hunting site, spread over at least a 10-by-10-foot area and scattered directly on the ground. Baiting and feeding are banned across the entire Lower Peninsula, public and private land alike, with a narrow exception for qualifying hunters with disabilities during the Liberty and Independence hunts. The DNR also recommends not re-baiting the same point on the ground, to limit disease transmission.
What happened to the UP antlerless drawing and DMUs 351 and 352?
Both are gone as of 2026. The Natural Resources Commission eliminated the Upper Peninsula antlerless access drawing and rescinded DMUs 351 and 352. Universal antlerless licenses can now only be used in the southernmost UP units — DMUs 022, 055, 121, 122, 155 and 255 — and each carries a use limit: one in DMU 022, two in DMUs 121, 122, 155 and 255, and five in DMU 055. Everywhere else in the UP is closed to antlerless harvest on a universal license. Those DMU boundaries split counties, so check the DNR's interactive map for the ground you actually hunt.
Do I have to report my deer, and how long do I have?
Yes, reporting is mandatory for every successful deer hunter. You have 72 hours from harvest, or until you transfer possession — hand it to a processor, a taxidermist or a buddy — whichever comes first. Report through the eLicense harvest report page or the Michigan DNR Hunt Fish app, and keep the confirmation number: that number is your proof, and a conservation officer can verify it in the field. Failing to report is a civil infraction carrying a fine of up to $150. You also cannot submit a head for CWD or TB testing until the harvest is reported.
Should I get my UP deer tested for CWD?
For 2026 the DNR's focused CWD surveillance is in Ionia, Kent, Mecosta, Montcalm and Newaygo counties — none of them in the UP — and focused bovine TB surveillance is in twelve northern Lower Peninsula counties. That said, CWD has been confirmed in free-ranging deer in Dickinson County, and for any county outside the focused surveillance areas the DNR provides free lymph node submission kits so you can have your deer tested anyway. Deer showing CWD-like symptoms are accepted statewide, year-round. Results come back at the DNR disease lab portal.
Can I use a crossbow during UP late archery?
This changed for 2026. Crossbows have always been legal in the UP during early archery (Oct. 1 – Nov. 14) and during firearm season. During late archery (Dec. 1 – Jan. 1) they were previously limited to hunters holding a disability bow permit — and as of Sept. 12, 2026 resident senior hunters with a valid senior deer, senior deer combo or senior universal antlerless license may also use one.
Where can I hunt if I don't have private land?
The UP is the best public-land hunting in the state and it isn't close. Between state forest, the Hiawatha and Ottawa national forests, and Commercial Forest Act land — private timber land enrolled in a tax program that requires public foot access for hunting and fishing — the walk-in acreage is enormous. Start with the DNR's state forest road maps for legal vehicle access, the national forest motor vehicle use maps for federal ground, and the GEMS network if you're after grouse.
Do you predict the rut?
No, and we're not going to. Moon illumination is shown on this page because hunters want to see it, not because it's a movement predictor — the research on lunar phase and deer movement doesn't support the claim. What does move deer is the calendar, cold fronts and pressure, and you can read all three off the conditions panel above without anyone selling you a score.
