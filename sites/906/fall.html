How this estimate works — and what it isn't. There is no live foliage sensor network in Michigan, so nothing on this page is an observation. It's a model: a date-driven progression curve for each color band, shifted by whether that band's overnight lows are running colder or warmer than normal for the date. Cold nights are the strongest weather trigger for color development, so a genuine cold snap pulls peak earlier and a warm stretch pushes it later — while an ordinary autumn leaves the estimate where it started. Treat it as a planning tool, then confirm against the UP Travel color reports before you drive four hours for it.

🍎 Cider Mills, Orchards & Fall Farms

Apples and cider in the UP

Straight talk: the UP is not cider-mill country the way southern Michigan is. There is no comprehensive UP cider-mill directory because there aren't enough mills to fill one. What the UP does have is a small cluster of real working orchards — concentrated in Menominee and Delta counties where the growing season is longest — plus farmers markets in nearly every town selling apples, cider and baked goods in September and October. These are the stops we can actually verify.

Always check before you drive. Orchard hours, U-pick availability and cider supply change week to week and sell out early in a short season — several of these are small family operations with no fixed schedule. Every listing below links to a source you can check the same day, and none of them display hours here, because published hours in the UP go stale fast.
Stops Near the Color

Pair a farm stop with a color drive

The UP's orchards sit in the southern counties, not up on the color routes — so pair them with a US-2 or US-41 approach day rather than trying to work one into a Keweenaw loop.

US-41 / M-35 — Escanaba to Marquette
Barron Orchards in Rock is the one central-UP farm stop, roughly halfway between Escanaba and Marquette.
US-41 South — Menominee County approach
Starz Orchard (Wallace) and Sunny G. Orchard (Stephenson) are the first real orchards north of the Wisconsin line — a natural first stop coming up from Green Bay.
Keweenaw & western UP
No orchards on these routes. Hit the Houghton, Calumet or Ontonagon farmers markets for apples and cider, or stock up before you head north.
Eastern UP — Tahquamenon & the Straits
Also orchard-free. If you want the full cider-mill-and-donut experience, it's over the Bridge — see the Up North fall guide for nine orchards within an hour of the Mackinac Bridge.

Frequently Asked

UP Fall Color FAQ

When do fall colors peak in the Upper Peninsula?
Broadly, the last week of September through the first week of October, running west to east: the western UP and interior highlands turn first, the Keweenaw and central UP next, and the eastern UP and Straits area last — often into mid-October. Lakeshore stretches lag the interior by several days because Superior and Michigan keep the shore warmer. The band cards above narrow that down using each band's actual overnight lows.
How much earlier is the UP than the rest of Michigan?
Roughly a week to ten days ahead of northern Lower Michigan, and two to three weeks ahead of southeast Michigan. That's the whole appeal of a UP color trip — you can catch peak up here and still catch a second peak downstate a couple of weekends later.
Are there cider mills in the Upper Peninsula?
Very few in the traditional sense. Starz Orchard near Wallace is the strongest verified UP stop for fresh-pressed apple cider, and a handful of orchards in Menominee and Delta counties sell apples and seasonal farm products. Most UP towns get their cider and cider donuts through farmers markets and local bakeries rather than a dedicated mill. If a full cider-mill day is the point of the trip, it's an easier day over the Mackinac Bridge — see our Up North fall guide.
What's the single best fall color drive in the UP?
Brockway Mountain Drive above Copper Harbor is the postcard — a ridge-top road with Lake Superior on one side and unbroken hardwood canopy on the other. If you want a full day instead of a viewpoint, the Black River Scenic Byway near Bessemer stacks five waterfalls into fourteen miles of color, and the M-123 loop through Tahquamenon and Paradise is the best eastern-UP option once the west has already dropped.
Does a cold snap really move peak color?
Yes — a run of cool nights above freezing, paired with bright days, is what drives the sugar trapping that produces the reds. That's why the estimate above adjusts on cold-night count. What actually damages a season is a hard early freeze or a big wind-and-rain event at peak, either of which can strip a band in a couple of days.
Do I need a Recreation Passport?
For state parks and DNR trailhead lots, yes — Tahquamenon Falls, the Porcupine Mountains, Fort Wilkins and Van Riper all require it. It's an annual add-on to your Michigan plate, or a day pass for out-of-state vehicles. Roadside pullouts on scenic byways and national forest land don't need one.
