How installing works

Every widget installs the same way. Paste the placeholder where the widget should appear and the loader script anywhere on the page — it finds the placeholder itself, and one script handles any number of widgets. 

<div data-906-widget="ski" data-client="your-id"></div>
<script async src="https://widgets.906dashboard.com/widget.js"></script>

Your client id carries your brand colours, your default location and your preferred size, so the snippet above is usually the whole job. The per-widget sections below list the attributes worth overriding.

Your domain has to be allowlisted first. Embedding is gated by a frame-ancestors header on our side. Until your hostname is on that list the widget renders as blank space — send us the address and it is a one-line change. Include any staging or preview host too: frame-ancestors applies to every frame in the chain, so a CMS page-builder preview that wraps your page needs listing as well as the live site.

If your CMS strips script tags

Some templates escape <script> inside a content block. If the snippet renders as visible text instead of a widget, use an iframe instead — same widget, no script tag. Swap ski.html for aurora.html or hunting.html as needed.

One trade-off worth knowing: without the script there is nothing to report the widget's height back to your page, so this version is a fixed-size card rather than the automatic band-on-desktop, card-on-mobile. It is pinned to size=card deliberately — a card is the size that works at every width. 

<iframe src="https://widgets.906dashboard.com/ski.html?client=your-id&size=card"
        title="Snow report — 906 Dashboard"
        width="100%" height="400" loading="lazy"
        referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin"
        style="border:0;max-width:360px"></iframe>

⛷️ Snow report

The headline is the base depth, because "is there anything to ski" is what decides whether somebody makes the drive. Below it: what fell in the last 24 hours, what is forecast over the next three days, and the temperature and wind at the hill.

Two scales, and the widget says which is which. Base depth and observed snowfall come from the nearest National Weather Service climate station — Michigan has only three, so that number is regional and the widget names the station under it. The three-day forecast is the hill's own. We label it rather than implying a summit reading, because a widget that sends someone up a bare mountain is worse than no widget. 
<div data-906-widget="ski" data-client="your-id"></div>
<script async src="https://widgets.906dashboard.com/widget.js"></script>

When a feed goes down

This sits on your homepage, so a dead upstream should be our problem rather than your phone call. Three tiers, in order: live data; then the last good reading that visitor saw, stamped Last known with a timestamp; then a complete, correctly-branded component saying conditions are unavailable. Never a broken box or an empty frame. It recovers on its own.

Checking it worked

Load your page and confirm:

  • the widget shows real numbers, not dashes
  • it resizes to fit rather than scrolling inside its own box
  • at phone width it reflows instead of pushing the page sideways

If you see an empty space where the widget should be, your domain almost certainly is not on the allowlist yet — the browser console will say so explicitly, naming frame-ancestors. The question we will ask is not "what is your domain" but what is in the address bar when you see it fail, because a CMS preview answers that differently from the live page.
