📋 Verified against DNR sources on Aug 23, 2026 · Rules change; re-check before opening day
2026 changed real things in the Lower Peninsula. The limited firearms deer zone was eliminated — all legal firearms are usable statewide as of Sept. 12, 2026. The Late Antlerless Firearm season now opens the Monday after December Firearm and runs to Jan. 1, the Extended Late Antlerless and January Archery seasons were eliminated entirely, and universal antlerless license use limits are now set county by county. Season So Far

Harvest and wildlife numbers

Michigan's mandatory harvest reporting gives a near-real-time picture of the deer season, and the DNR's grouse and woodcock cooperator survey gives something even better for small game: county-level flushes per hour, reported by hunters, updated as the season runs.

We link to the DNR's own numbers rather than republishing them. Reported harvest totals are preliminary — they are what hunters have entered so far, not the DNR's final harvest estimate, and they lag the actual kill by up to 72 hours because that's the reporting window. We are not scraping eLicense pages to build our own tallies. If the DNR makes a supported feed available, we'll ask first and wire it in properly.

Frequently Asked

Northern Michigan Hunting FAQ

What are legal hunting hours in Northern Michigan?
For deer, bear, fall turkey, furbearers and most small game: one-half hour before sunrise to one-half hour after sunset. Woodcock is different — sunrise to sunset. Regular waterfowl is different again — one-half hour before sunrise to sunset, with no evening half hour. Early teal is sunrise to sunset. The whole northern Lower Peninsula is on Eastern Time, so the DNR's printed Zone A table applies directly, with the zone-offset adjustment for your longitude. The card at the top of this page computes all of it for whichever county you pick.
Can I bait deer in the northern Lower Peninsula?
No. Baiting and feeding are banned across the entire Lower Peninsula, public and private land alike. The only exception is for hunters who meet the disability qualifications during the Liberty and Independence hunts. Food plots are a different thing and remain legal on private land — naturally occurring foods, standing agricultural crops and food placed through normal agricultural practices are not considered bait. Scent-based attractants are allowed if they are made inaccessible to deer, and cervid urine lures are restricted to certified CWD-tested products.
How many antlerless licenses can I use in my county?
Use limits are now set county by county in the Lower Peninsula. Across the northern Lower: three in Antrim, Benzie, Charlevoix, Cheboygan, Crawford, Emmet, Grand Traverse, Kalkaska, Lake, Leelanau, Otsego, Roscommon and Wexford; four in Clare and Gladwin; five in Iosco, Manistee, Mason, Missaukee, Ogemaw and Osceola; and ten in Alcona, Alpena, Montmorency, Oscoda and Presque Isle. The DNR now reviews and publishes these limits annually, so treat any number you remember from last year as suspect. Pick your county above and this page shows yours.
Do I have to report my deer, and how long do I have?
Yes, reporting is mandatory for every successful deer hunter. You have 72 hours from harvest, or until you transfer possession — hand it to a processor, a taxidermist or a buddy — whichever comes first. Report through the eLicense harvest report page or the Michigan DNR Hunt Fish app, and keep the confirmation number: that number is your proof, and a conservation officer can verify it in the field. Failing to report is a civil infraction carrying a fine of up to $150. You also cannot submit a head for CWD or TB testing until the harvest is reported.
Which counties are disease-testing priorities in 2026?
Bovine TB focused surveillance for 2026 covers twelve counties, and most of them are up here: Alcona, Alpena, Charlevoix, Cheboygan, Crawford, Iosco, Montmorency, Ogemaw, Oscoda, Otsego, Presque Isle and Roscommon. If you hunt one of those, submitting a head genuinely matters to the state's surveillance goals. Focused CWD surveillance for 2026 is further south — Ionia, Kent, Mecosta, Montcalm and Newaygo — though CWD has been confirmed in free-ranging deer in Gladwin and Ogemaw counties. Outside a focus county you can still get a deer tested with a free lymph node submission kit.
What is changing in 2027?
Lower Peninsula only, and it's substantial. Starting with the 2027 seasons a single deer license will be valid for one deer with at least three antler points on one side, or for one antlerless deer. A combo license will allow one antlered and one antlerless deer, or two antlerless. Existing DMU-specific antler point restrictions stay in place on top of that. The commission also approved an "earn a second buck" pilot in southern Zone 3, where a hunter must take an antlerless deer before becoming eligible for a second buck under a four-point restriction. Upper Peninsula regulations are unchanged in 2027.
Where can I hunt if I don't have private land?
Up North is a mixed-ownership landscape, so access takes more planning than it does in the UP. Your three levers are state forest land, the Huron-Manistee National Forest, and the Hunting Access Program — private land whose owners are paid to open it to public hunting. Start with HAP if you're hunting the agricultural fringe, the national forest motor vehicle use maps if you're on federal ground, and the state forest road maps everywhere for what you can legally drive.
Do you predict the rut?
No, and we're not going to. Moon illumination is shown on this page because hunters want to see it, not because it's a movement predictor — the research on lunar phase and deer movement doesn't support the claim. What does move deer is the calendar, cold fronts and pressure, and you can read all three off the conditions panel above without anyone selling you a score.
